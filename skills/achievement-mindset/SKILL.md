# Achievement Mindset (Think and Grow Rich, used honestly)

Hill's 1937 classic mixes durable achievement psychology with some unscientific, metaphysical claims. Take the **practical, evidence-aligned core**; skip the magical thinking (`critical-reasoning` applies).

## The durable principles
- **Definiteness of purpose (Desire):** one clearly-defined major goal beats vague wishes. Hill's prompt — write exactly what you want, by when, what you'll give for it, and your plan; review it daily. Maps directly to his Sunday "Goal setting for next $1M" line.
- **Written, specific, time-bound goals with a plan** — the actionable core, and consistent with modern goal-setting research (specific + challenging goals beat "do your best").
- **Organized planning:** convert desire into concrete plans; when a plan fails, revise the **plan**, never the goal.
- **Decision:** decide promptly once informed, and reverse slowly and rarely (pairs with `decision-intelligence` on reversibility).
- **Persistence:** the real differentiator. Most quit near the breakthrough ("three feet from gold"). Persistence is built from definiteness + plans + a supportive circle, not willpower alone.
- **Mastermind:** a trusted circle of aligned minds multiplies capability and accountability — his networking-goals line and reconnect-list put to work.
- **Autosuggestion / belief (pragmatic read):** self-talk and belief shape persistence and action — useful as motivation engineering, not magic.
- **Specialized knowledge + imagination:** know your domain deeply, then combine ideas creatively (`creative-ideation`).

## How to use for Nrupal
Sharpen the "next $1M" into a written, specific, dated goal with a plan; protect persistence through the dip; treat his network as a mastermind; review progress weekly (`weekly-accountability`).

## Anti-patterns to refuse
- Vague goals; magical thinking in place of plans + action; changing the goal at the first obstacle; going it alone instead of building a mastermind; motivation with no system behind it (`consistency`).
