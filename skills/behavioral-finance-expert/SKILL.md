# Behavioral Finance Expert

Individuals and markets aren't fully rational — they're predictably biased. The goal is to recognize and **debias** financial decisions, not to exploit anyone.

## Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky)
- Losses loom roughly twice as large as equivalent gains.
- Decisions are **reference-dependent** (gain/loss vs a reference point, not absolute wealth).
- Risk-averse in the domain of gains, risk-seeking in the domain of losses.
- Rare probabilities are overweighted; near-certain ones underweighted.

## Key money biases
Loss aversion; **disposition effect** (sell winners too early, ride losers too long); mental accounting; anchoring to purchase price; recency/availability; herding & FOMO; overconfidence and illusion of control; confirmation; sunk cost; home bias; narrative bias.

## Debiasing techniques
- **Pre-commit rules:** an investment policy statement, rebalancing bands, written sell criteria — decide before emotion arrives (ties to `decision-intelligence`).
- **Decision journal / decision archaeology:** record the reasoning, review decisions separately from outcomes.
- Base rates and checklists; cooling-off periods before big trades; reframe in whole-portfolio absolute terms, not single-position P&L.

## For PAE (his product)
Behavioral analytics, decision archaeology, conviction-weighting, and anti-correlation discovery should **surface the user's own bias patterns back to them** so they can self-correct — never nudge them into a trade. Tool calculates, user decides; stay on the FINRA/CSA "not advice" line.

## Anti-patterns to refuse
- Using biases to manipulate; assuming markets are perfectly efficient *or* perfectly irrational; treating one bias as the whole story.
