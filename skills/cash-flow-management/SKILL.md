# Cash-Flow Management

Profit is opinion; cash is fact. Forecast the timing of money, protect the reserve, and manage the levers — before the pinch, not during it.

## When to use
- Business or personal cash planning; runway checks.
- Seasonal flows; before big commitments or hires.
- When receivables run late and outflows bunch up.

## Method
1. **Map the flows**: recurring in/out, seasonal patterns, one-offs — and the **lag structure** (payment terms, receivable days, settlement delays). Timing is the whole game.
2. **13-week rolling forecast**: weekly buckets for near-term cash; monthly/annual for planning; update weekly — stale forecasts cause surprises only.
3. **Levers, used honestly**: accelerate inflows (invoicing discipline, deposits, follow-up cadence), phase outflows (terms, batch purchases, timing) — never by stiffing obligations quietly.
4. **Reserve targets**: minimum cash = X months of fixed costs (size to volatility); held separately from operating; only breached by explicit decision.
5. **Working capital**: AR aging review on schedule (who owes what, chase ladder), AP calendar (discounts vs float), inventory only where it pays (`procurement-vendor-management` for terms).
6. **Runway math** (projects/business lines): burn = net monthly outflow; runway = cash ÷ burn; alert thresholds at 6/3 months.
7. **Stress it**: revenue −20%, collections +30 days late, surprise capex — does the plan hold? Record answers + responses.
8. **Report**: weekly cash position + short forecast; monthly summary with drivers annotated.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Rolling forecast maintained weekly; lag assumptions documented.
- [ ] Reserve target set and separated; breaches require explicit decisions.
- [ ] AR aging reviewed on cadence; chase ladder applied.
- [ ] Stress cases run and responses pre-decided.

## Red lines
- No commitments against expected receivables that haven't landed; reserves never put at risk; never use vendors as silent financing.
