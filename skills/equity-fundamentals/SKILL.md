# Equity Fundamentals

Own businesses, not tickers. Read the statements, check the quality of earnings, and verify the story against numbers — in that order.

## When to use
- Researching a company for investment (selection).
- Sanity-checking a pitch, article, or advisor recommendation.
- Annual/biannual reviews of held positions.

## Method
1. **Three statements + cash conversion**: income, balance sheet, cash flow over 5+ years; net income vs operating cash flow (accrual check); free cash flow after real capex.
2. **Quality metrics with context**: ROIC (not just ROE), margin trends (direction matters), debt/EBITDA + interest coverage, working-capital discipline; compare to the company's own history and peers.
3. **Capital allocation**: dilution vs buybacks, dividends covered by FCF, acquisitions rational or empire-building; management track record vs promises.
4. **Moat evidence (not claims)**: pricing power (did they raise prices through inflation?), switching costs, network/scale; a moat is a number pattern, not a story.
5. **Red flags checklist**: revenue recognition games, receivables/inventory growing faster than sales, frequent "one-offs", related-party deals, restatements, auditor churn; any flag → discount or pass.
6. **Management & incentives**: compensation tied to what (per-share or empire?), insider buying/selling patterns, candor in calls (do they admit misses?).
7. **Summary**: strengths / weaknesses / unknowns, each with evidence links; unknowns that are thesis-critical go to open questions.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Statements reviewed for 5+ years; cash conversion checked.
- [ ] Red-flag checklist passed or flags documented with mitigations.
- [ ] Moat claims backed by data (prices, margins, share).
- [ ] Every thesis-critical claim sourced.

## Red lines
- No investing off summaries/screens alone; never ignore dilution/debt; no "great story" without numbers.
