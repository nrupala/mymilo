# First-Principles Thinking

Reason **up from fundamental truths**, not sideways from analogy. Reasoning by analogy copies what others do with slight tweaks; first-principles reasoning asks what is actually, irreducibly true and rebuilds from there. Most "impossible" or "that's just how it's done" walls are conventions, not laws.

## Process
1. **State the problem or claim plainly.** Strip the jargon and the inherited framing.
2. **Break it into fundamentals.** Decompose to the most basic verified truths — physics, math, material/commodity costs, hard regulatory or contractual constraints. These are the bedrock.
3. **Challenge every assumption.** For each "requirement," ask: is this a *law* (physics, regulation, math) or a *convention* (habit, legacy, analogy)? Conventions are negotiable; laws are not.
4. **Rebuild upward** from the fundamentals to a solution, deliberately ignoring "the way it's done."

## Tools
- **The boil-down test:** keep asking "is this actually true / actually required, or just inherited?" until you hit something irreducible.
- **Idiot index:** cost of the finished thing ÷ cost of its raw materials. A high ratio means you're paying for inefficiency, not for physics — that gap is the opportunity.
- **Constraint sort:** explicitly list which constraints are immovable (physical limits, FINRA/CSA, electrical safety, crypto guarantees) vs which are assumptions you can dissolve.

## When to use
Stuck problems; "impossible" costs/timelines; consensus "can't be done"; clean-sheet designs; or any time the obvious approach is just the inherited one.

## When NOT to overuse
Well-trodden, low-stakes problems where analogy is faster and good enough. Don't reinvent settled engineering for its own sake — and in safety-critical work (Nrupal's domain), never dissolve a constraint that physics or regulation actually requires.

For Nrupal specifically: on PAE / Guardian Mesh / Aetheris, separate the genuine hard constraints (cryptographic guarantees, FINRA 2214 / CSA, the physics of the machine) from the inherited assumptions, and rebuild only the latter.
