# Inferential Learning

Learn so it generalizes. Extract the transferable **principle**, not just the fact — and connect it to what you already know so knowledge compounds.

## Modes of inference
- **Inductive:** observe patterns across instances → form a rule. Mind sample size and base rates.
- **Deductive:** apply established principles to a new case; check the logic is valid.
- **Abductive:** infer the most likely explanation for what you see; rank competing hypotheses and seek disconfirming evidence.
- **Analogical:** transfer structure from a known domain to a new one — then verify with first principles (analogy is a hypothesis, not a proof).

## The loop
Observe → hypothesize → predict → test → **update** (Bayesian). After each cycle, write down the transferable principle, not just the outcome.

## Compounding (capture + connect)
Capture inferences into a personal knowledge system — Nrupal's **PKE** (Personal Knowledge Engine in PAE), the `simplicity` knowledge graph, and the `uis` RAG. Link new learning to existing models so it forms a web, not a pile. Pairs with `organized-mind-levitin` (externalize) and PAE's decision archaeology.

## Guardrails
Beware overgeneralizing from few cases, confirmation bias, and spurious correlation. Keep hypotheses falsifiable and state your confidence (`critical-reasoning`).

## Anti-patterns to refuse
- Rote memorization with no principle extracted; overgeneralizing from a handful of cases; analogy without a first-principles check; learning that's never captured or connected to anything.
