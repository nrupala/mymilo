# Mental Models — Charlie Munger

Worldly wisdom comes from a **latticework of models** drawn from many disciplines, not one. "To a man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail." Run important problems through several disciplines' models, invert, and scan for the standard human misjudgments.

## The latticework (models worth carrying)
- **Math:** compounding, probability, permutations/combinations, expected value, decision trees, basic statistics.
- **Physics / engineering:** equilibrium, critical mass, breakpoints, backup systems, **margin of safety**.
- **Biology / evolution:** adaptation, natural selection, ecosystems, niches.
- **Economics:** supply & demand, competitive advantage / moats, economies of scale, specialization, tragedy of the commons.
- **Psychology:** the misjudgment tendencies below.

## Three habits
- **Invert, always invert.** Solve forward and backward — ask "how would this fail?" and avoid that. (Often easier to find the answer by eliminating the dumb.)
- **Circle of competence.** Know its edge. Inside it, act; outside it, be humble or pass. Avoid being wrong about the big things.
- **Watch for the lollapalooza effect.** When several models or forces push the same direction at once, outcomes go nonlinear — for good or ill. Look for confluence.

## Munger maxims
Incentives dominate behavior; avoid intense ideology; spend more time thinking and reading than acting; avoid the big, unrecoverable mistakes; invert.
