# Negotiation

Two layers: the **fundamentals** you prepare, and the **improvisation** you do live. Michael Wheeler's insight (*The Art of Negotiation*) is that real negotiations are chaotic and nonlinear — you need a sense of direction AND the agility to adapt, like a jazz musician, not a rigid script.

## Fundamentals (prepare before the table)
- **BATNA** — your Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement. Know it and strengthen it; it is your real walk-away power.
- **Reservation price + ZOPA** — your walk-away number, and the Zone of Possible Agreement where yours and theirs overlap.
- **Interests, not positions** — negotiate the underlying interest, not the stated demand. Ask *why* they want what they ask for.
- **Create value before claiming it** — most deals aren't zero-sum. Trade across issues you each value differently to expand the pie, then divide.

## Improvisation (Wheeler — the live game)
- **A sense of direction, not a script** ("a map of the Pyrenees"): a plan orients you even when the terrain differs. Carry a **Plan B** (and C).
- **Presence of mind** — manage your own emotional state; stay composed (pairs with `emotional-intelligence-manager`).
- **Situational awareness** — read shifting interests, constraints, and emotions in real time (pairs with `psychological-analyst`).
- **Openings → critical moments → closing** — anchor thoughtfully when you have information, recognize the pivot points, and close cleanly.
- **Debrief every negotiation** — negotiation is a "wicked learning environment" where feedback misleads; review deliberately to actually learn.

## Tactics & guardrails
Anchor first when informed; make their "yes" easy; separate the people from the problem; hard on the problem, soft on the person; aim for "fair enough" agreements that hold.
