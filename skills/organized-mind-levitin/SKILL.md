# The Organized Mind (Daniel J. Levitin)

Organized people aren't better at remembering — they **offload** remembering onto the external world so the brain is free to think.

## Externalize memory
Get it out of your head and into a single **trusted system** (notes, lists, calendar). The brain is for having ideas, not holding them. This directly fixes Nrupal's "reflection is scheduled and he reads heavily, but nothing is captured" gap — build a reading list / notes / decision journal.

## The attentional system
Attention is finite, with a "central executive" (focused) mode and a "mind-wandering" (default) mode that compete for the same resource. Protect focused attention by removing distractions; **schedule mind-wandering deliberately** (his reflection blocks earn their place).

## Decision fatigue
Every decision — trivial or weighty — draws on the same finite pool. Reduce trivial decisions through routines, defaults, and standard work, preserving capacity for the decisions that matter.

## Active sorting & categories
Give everything a home, and choose categories that match how you'll **retrieve** them, not how they look. A bounded "junk drawer" is fine; an overflowing undecided pile is not. Process inboxes toward zero.

## Satisfice the small stuff
"Good enough" for low-stakes choices conserves the budget for high-stakes ones. Maximize only where it counts.

## Tie-in for Nrupal
Feed his **Life Areas Map** (8 lanes) and the **Build Your Life scorecard**; close the reading-capture gap with a real capture habit. Pairs with `calendar-organized-human` (the calendar is the external memory for time) and `weekly-accountability`.

## Anti-patterns to refuse
- Keeping it all in your head; over-categorizing into a taxonomy nobody can use; maximizing every trivial choice; running multiple competing systems instead of one trusted one.
