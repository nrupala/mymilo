# Portfolio Construction

The portfolio is the decision, not the individual pick. Set policy first, size positions so no single outcome decides your year, and write the rebalancing rules before you need them.

## When to use
- Designing or revising the overall allocation.
- Deciding position sizes for new holdings.
- Annual policy reviews; after major life/income changes.

## Method
1. **Policy first**: goals, horizon, risk capacity (real ability to take losses), liquidity needs, constraints (concentration from employer/equity comp) — written down.
2. **Asset roles**: growth / income / inflation-hedge / ballast / cash — each holding must have a job; if two holdings do the same job, one may go.
3. **Diversification math, honestly**: correlations (and their tendency to converge in crises); diversification is imperfect but still the only free lunch — use it with open eyes.
4. **Position sizing rules**: max % per single name, per sector/theme, per geography; risk-aware sizing (fixed-fraction or volatility-aware); write the rules so sizing isn't a mood.
5. **Drawdown planning**: set expected drawdown ranges in advance; pre-decide the response (rebalance, add, or hold — not panic).
6. **Rebalancing policy**: calendar (e.g., quarterly) or threshold bands (±X%); tax-aware in taxable accounts (use contributions first); written rule beats improvisation.
7. **Stress scenarios**: rates up, recession, sector shock, currency — check portfolio behavior, not just holdings.
8. **Policy statement**: one page — allocation targets, sizing rules, rebalance policy, review cadence; it is the contract you sign with yourself.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Written policy statement: allocation, sizing rules, rebalance rule, reviews.
- [ ] Concentration limits explicit (single name/sector/theme).
- [ ] Drawdown expectations + responses pre-decided.
- [ ] Stress scenarios run and recorded.

## Red lines
- No unbounded leverage; no concentration beyond written limits without a written reason; no drifting allocation without updating policy first.
